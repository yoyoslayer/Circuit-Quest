# Review 5: external research (key `research-external`)

**Scope.** This file collects outside benchmarks for Circuit Crew: (a) Good Job! and other physics-comedy games, (b) games and simulations that teach electronics or systems, and the research on student misconceptions, (c) guidance on stylised toon props. From these it sets out **checkable design rules**, each with a "how to check in this game" line. It ends with a few findings I confirmed on real screenshots.

**Method and caveats.** The research used web search (standard mode) plus full-page fetches of the Nintendo Life review, the Nintendo World Report review and the Nintendo Everything interview with Paladin. Twinfinite returned 403, so its points come from search excerpts. `docs/RESEARCH_good_job_nintendo.md` already covers Nintendo's level-design sources: kishōtenketsu, World 1-1, Captain Toad, Kirby's rule that a hit counts if it looks like one. This file does not repeat them; it adds the education-research and art-direction side. Every claim attributed to a reviewer is paraphrased.

## A. Benchmarks: what physics-comedy players praise and complain about

| Game | Praised | Criticised | Lesson for Circuit Crew |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Good Job!** (Paladin/Nintendo 2020, Metacritic 78) | Many ways to finish each job. Slapstick chaos ("electronics as slingshots"). Varied objectives on every floor. Grades give a reason to replay (NL 9/10, NWR 9/10, Cubed3 9/10). | **Fixed isometric camera hides objectives** (NWR, NL). Fiddly controls (NWR). "Best of three" grading feels odd, since wrecking the office can still earn an A (NWR). Some stages run much longer than others (NWR). Things get stuck (NL). Too many physics objects in the way, glitchy collision (TSA 7/10). It doesn't explain how things work, so a player thought it was bugged when they had just missed a mechanic (Twinfinite). NPCs barely react to chaos (NL). | Camera control and occlusion fade (rule 8) answer the #1 complaint. Re-check every room at every lens. "Looks broken but isn't" is the worst failure for a no-text game, so every blocked state needs a visible *cause*. |
| **Good Job! dev process** (Nintendo Everything interview) | Prototype the core verb → build the **non-destructive "problem"** (the hardest part) → add destructive shortcuts. Never tell the player "you can't do that". Tuned for solo first. Features judged in a day on whether they had a "spark". | They tried overcharged cables that turned printers into bombs and moved away from it; the mundane office made the humour land better. | This is AGENTS rule 5, and it is validated. Keep electricity mundane and believable; the comedy comes from the physics, not from fantasy electricity. |
| **Untitled Goose Game** | A to-do list with *no hints on how*; the list keeps you exploring. Each area opens as a sandbox. "Conflict is always easy to read" (Disseldorp). The art is "very clean, silhouette", with no camouflage detail (McMaster). | Later goals get abstract and hard to decipher. | State the goal as *what* and never *how*. Readability comes from clean silhouettes on flat colour, not from detail. |
| **Human: Fall Flat** | One obvious solution plus at least one absurd alternative per obstacle. Emergence from familiar physics: gravity, inertia, grabbing. | Clumsy control is part of the joke, but it frustrates. | Every room needs an *obvious* clean route and at least one *absurd* cable or slingshot route that also works. |
| **Overcooked** | Tasks "understood at a glance". The level design is where the challenge lives; the layout changes. Iterated with playtesters. A timer won out over lives. | (n/a) | Short, readable tasks. Difficulty comes from **layout**, not from rules text. A timer beats failure states. |
| **Moving Out** | An assist mode for nearly every mechanic: longer timers, delivered items vanish, skippable levels. | "Dull solo experience". Getting stuck on the environment. | A physics game must be fun **solo**. Offer assists: longer time, a looser cable limit, skip a station. |
| **Totally Reliable Delivery Service** (OpenCritic 51) | Funny physics. | "Aimless world", janky controls, basic missions, glitchy. | Physics comedy without tight goals and reliable controls scores badly. Chaos needs a job to be chaos *against*. |
| **Zachtronics** (SpaceChem, Opus Magnum, Shenzhen I/O) | Open-ended solutions. The puzzle is solved once the output is produced, *then* metrics (cost, cycles, area) and histograms against other players. "A puzzling task" rather than a trick. | (n/a) | Matches the works / reliable / elegant tiers. Show the *player's own* metric next to a target after a solve; never hide the pass behind the optimum. |
| **The Incredible Machine** | Real simulation, so many solutions, some unforeseen by the designers. Puzzles made by building a machine, removing parts and adding decoys. | Odd physics weakens the educational claim (HG101). | Station solvers prove solvability. Decoy parts are fine, but the simulation must stay truthful or the lesson is lost. |
| **Turing Tumble** | 51–60 puzzles graded in difficulty; each one introduces a concept reused later; a comic frames it. | (n/a) | One new concept per job, reused in the next. Seven stations already do this; check that each job reuses the last job's idea. |
| **PhET Circuit Construction Kit** | Implicit scaffolding through affordances, productive constraints and *showing invisible phenomena*; immediate dynamic feedback. In a study, groups with and without the current visualisation did equally well on exams. | (n/a) | Visualising current helps feel and debugging, but it is not a substitute for the loop itself being the mechanic. Visual vocabulary must be consistent (see misconception M2 and finding F2). |
| **Kerbal Space Program** in classrooms | Orbital mechanics become tangible. Students report satisfaction mixed with "frustration at the restrictions of physics". | (n/a) | Truthful physics that frustrates is fine **if** failure is legible and cheap to retry. |

**Research on learning games**

- **Intrinsic integration** (Habgood & Ainsworth 2011, *Zombie Division*). Children learned more from the version where the maths *was* the combat, and chose to play it about **7× longer** in free time than the version where maths was a reward gate. → The electronics must *be* the action (where the cable goes, which block goes in the chain), never a quiz between actions.
- **Failure-improvement cycle** (Juul, *The Art of Failure*): goal → failure exposes a gap → experiment → fix. Players prefer failures they feel *responsible* for. Failures with no legible cause (E.T. on the 2600 is the classic case) cause quitting. → Every fail state must say *what physically happened*, not "wrong".
- **Seductive details and coherence** (Mayer; Rey 2012 and Sundararajan & Adesope 2020 meta-analyses). Interesting but irrelevant material lowers retention and transfer by adding extraneous load. → Flavour text on bench cards and decorative numbers near the lesson cost learning. Keep flavour in the room, out of the lesson.
- **Analogy limits.** The water-pipe analogy "if taken too far … can create misconceptions" (it has no magnetic field and the resistance doesn't map). Teacher notes warn that a poorly chosen analogy teaches the wrong thing. → Any visual metaphor (beads, glow, "heat") needs a check against the misconceptions below.

**Art direction for stylised toon props**

- Untitled Goose Game: low-poly meshes, flat colours, untextured models, "very clean, silhouette", no camouflage-style detail. Readability comes from shape and colour blocks.
- Animal Crossing: New Horizons: the furniture team made *hundreds* of pieces to read at Switch resolution, and set **one consistent height scale** for indoor and outdoor furniture (developer presentation).
- General stylised-prop practice (80.lv, prop-art checklists): block out primary forms and proportions for **silhouette at game-camera distance** before any detail. Colour, material and lighting carry more than geometry in low-poly. Bevel or chamfer the main edges so the toon ramp and outline pick up form. Check scale against a human reference.
- Juice (Jonasson & Purho, "Juice it or lose it", 2012; Nijman, "The Art of Screenshake", 2013): the same mechanic feels alive with layered particles, sound, squash and shake. Folmer Kelly's counterpoint: over-polish can bury information. → The rule is "restrained glow": juice must never cover the thing being taught (see F4).

## B. Design rules (checkable)

ID format: **R-<group>-n**. Each rule has a source and a one-line **check** that can be run in this repo.

### FUN

- **R-FUN-1. Clean problem first, absurd shortcut second, both rewarded.** Source: Paladin interview; Human Fall Flat "obvious plus absurd". *Check:* for each room, the spec in `tests/*.spec.ts` must contain a tidy route test **and** a chaos-route test (slingshot or throw) that also reaches success. List any room that has only one.
- **R-FUN-2. Chaos needs a job to be chaos against.** Source: TRDS's "aimless world". *Check:* on a start shot (e.g. `research-external-11-meeting-start`), the goal object and its target can both be found within 10 s of free camera, and the objective card names a physical object and a place.
- **R-FUN-3. Something funny happens within 30 s of starting.** Source: Good Job! slingshot praise; "juice it". *Check:* scripted run (g.walk to the first prop, g.key('KeyE'), g.key('Space')) produces a tumble, spark or launch by `snap().time < 30`. Screenshot it.
- **R-FUN-4. The world reacts to chaos.** Source: NL's criticism of Good Job! NPCs. *Check:* knock a coworker's desk prop with a thrown item and screenshot 0.5 s later. There must be a visible coworker reaction (pose, emote, sound).
- **R-FUN-5. Level length stays even across rooms.** Source: NWR, "some take much longer". *Check:* compare the simulated finish times of the scripted clean solves in the specs. No room's clean solve should take more than 2× the median; flag any outlier.
- **R-FUN-6. No dead time.** No walk of more than about 8 s with nothing to touch, and no unskippable wait over 3 s. Source: Good Job! "large levels a chore" (GameSpot, in docs/RESEARCH); Overcooked's constant action. *Check:* in spec routes, measure the longest `walk()` leg (distance ÷ walk speed) and the longest `wait()` the player cannot act during (e.g. arcade soak 3.2 s, `tests/station-arcade.spec.ts:30`).
- **R-FUN-7. A grade gives a reason to replay without making chaos the optimum.** Source: NWR calls "best of three" odd. *Check:* in `src/sim/grade.ts:3`, confirm that a pure-chaos run cannot beat a clean run on *every* axis. The result card should show which axis earned the grade, so destruction-for-speed reads as a choice, not an exploit.

### PLAYABILITY

- **R-PLAY-1. Camera never hides the objective.** This was Good Job!'s #1 complaint. *Check:* for each level, `g.orbit(±400)` at zoom 9 and zoom 38. In every shot, the goal socket or machine is either visible or its walls/pillars are faded.
- **R-PLAY-2. "Looks in, is in".** If a plug looks seated from the current camera, it snaps (AGENTS rule 7; Kirby's camera-honest hit rule). *Check:* drop a plug 0.3 m short of a socket at the default lens and screenshot. It must snap or show a clear magnet cue.
- **R-PLAY-3. Nothing required can get stuck for good.** Source: Good Job! "things get stuck"; Moving Out "stuck on environment". *Check:* throw each required item into a corner or behind a fixture and advance 10 s; `snap()` shows it respawned. Run `g.audit()` on all levels (no start overlaps).
- **R-PLAY-4. Physics clutter never blocks the clean route.** Source: TSA, "too many physics objects in the way". *Check:* the spec's clean route still passes with clutter seeded (it does today). Also, the longest detour that clutter forces on the route should be under 1 m.
- **R-PLAY-5. Solo-complete, with assists.** Source: Nintendo requires solo fun; Moving Out assist mode. *Check:* the pause menu offers longer time, a looser cable limit, and skipping a station or job. If it doesn't, log it as missing.
- **R-PLAY-6. Retry is cheap.** Source: Juul; KSP frustration. *Check:* after any station failure, the time from the fail toast to the next "Power on"/"Pulse" input should be under 3 s, with no menu.

### TEACHING

- **R-TEACH-1. The mechanic *is* the concept** (intrinsic integration). *Check:* for each station, the action that fixes the fault is the physical act the concept describes: put a block *in the chain*, put a diode *across the coil*, move the pod *out of the brick's shadow*. Flag any concept taught only by picking from a list of values.
- **R-TEACH-2. Failure shows its cause in the world, then in one sentence.** Source: Juul; PhET "show invisible phenomena". *Check:* every fail toast (`grep station-toast`) is paired with a visible cause on screen: a glowing fuse (shot 04 ✓), a spike on the scope (shot 08 ✓). The toast says *what happened*, not what to do.
- **R-TEACH-3. One new concept per job, reused next job.** Source: Turing Tumble progression; kishōtenketsu. *Check:* list each job's new idea from the station spec. Job n+1 should require job n's idea plus exactly one new idea.
- **R-TEACH-4. Visual vocabulary is fixed.** One look means "current is flowing", and nothing else may use that look. Source: PhET representations; analogy-limit research. *Check:* grep all flowing-dot or bead effects (`src/game.ts:100` hint dots, garage and qfn beads). Each must mean current through a **closed loop** only (see F2).
- **R-TEACH-5. No false numbers.** Every number the player can read is the sim's own value, in the same units as the card. Source: AGENTS rule 10. *Check:* for each bench, compare the monitor values with `snap().station.reading` (e.g. arcade `iLoad` 13.6 mA, `tests/station-arcade.spec.ts:31`).
- **R-TEACH-6. Each analogy is labelled as a stand-in.** *Check:* stand-ins such as the "ribbon not to scale" (`src/stations/spectrum/station.ts:327`) say so on screen ✓. Apply the same to colour (see F3).
- **R-TEACH-7. Safety truth over safety theatre.** Hazard rules match real cause and effect, so kids aren't taught superstition (see M9 and F1). *Check:* every hazard rule in `src/sim/electrical.ts` and the runtimes has a one-line real-world justification in its comment.

### CLARITY

- **R-CLAR-1. Goal = what, never how.** Source: Untitled Goose Game. *Check:* objective-card strings contain a target object and place, and no instructions for a solution. Search the cards for "use", "put", and specific part values.
- **R-CLAR-2. Word budget.** Each screen shows at most about 25 words outside the bench monitor; flavour text fades after 4 s. Source: seductive details; AGENTS rule 2. *Check:* count visible words in the bench shots (F5 counts them).
- **R-CLAR-3. Show each value once.** Mayer's redundancy principle. *Check:* no value appears both on the in-world monitor and on the HUD card (F5: arcade shows "CHAIN 0 Ω" on the monitor and "Chain wire only" on the card).
- **R-CLAR-4. Prompts name real, available actions.** *Check:* every bottom prompt's key does something at that moment. Shot 04 shows "P · Fuse cooling", a key bound to a state rather than an action (F6).
- **R-CLAR-5. Hero and goal stand out from clutter.** Source: Goose "conflict easy to read". *Check:* desaturate shot 01 or 11 to greyscale. Pip, the held item and the goal marker should still be the three highest-contrast shapes.

### MODELS

- **R-MOD-1. Silhouette first.** Every interactable prop is identifiable from its black silhouette at zoom 22 (default walk distance). *Check:* render a level with `query:'norim'` and flat black materials (a debug flag), and name each prop from the shot.
- **R-MOD-2. One consistent scale.** Measure Pip's height (from `src/render/pipRig.ts`) and size every desk, chair, door and plug in proportion, exaggerating only gameplay parts (plugs, sockets, buttons) by a fixed factor (e.g. 1.5×). Source: Animal Crossing's single height scale. *Check:* line up props next to Pip in a gallery (`tools/debug/gallery.mjs`) and list the outliers.
- **R-MOD-3. Bevel the main edges.** Chamfer the primary edges (about 2–4% of the prop's size) so the 3-step ramp shows form. Pure boxes read as UI cards. *Check:* zoom to 9 on desks, carts and benches. Each flat-sided box should show a lit chamfer line.
- **R-MOD-4. No camouflage detail.** Detail belongs only where it carries information: labels on parts, band stripes on resistors. Source: McMaster. *Check:* at zoom 22, no prop has noisy decals that compete with the goal.
- **R-MOD-5. Glow never hides the taught part.** Source: Folmer Kelly; AGENTS rule 9. *Check:* during every spark, arc or flash event, the part it comes from stays visible in its outline (F4 fails this).
- **R-MOD-6. Contact is believable.** Nothing floats or sinks more than about 2 cm, and cables don't clip through props they wrap. *Check:* `g.audit(0.02)` plus close-ups at zoom 9 of cable wraps on fixtures.

## C. Electronics misconceptions the game must not reinforce

Sources: Duit et al.'s categories (current consumption, local reasoning, sequential reasoning, voltage in closed circuits); constant-current-battery studies (Dupin & Johsua 1987; IOP Spark summaries); Küçüközer & Kocakülah 2008 (38% "current is used up"); Gardon et al. 2021 (clashing current, shared current, current as water flow, short circuit; 69% of pre-service teachers could not explain a short circuit); BEST/STEM UK teacher notes on fuses, earthing and mains; AP Physics C 2021 chief reader report on capacitors; Tabor-Morris et al. on "radio waves are sound"; Libarkin et al. 2011 on UV/IR.

| # | Misconception | How it could slip into Circuit Crew | Guard / check |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Current is used up** by the load, so less current returns. | Cable beads that fade or slow after the machine. A "drained" look on the return side. | Bead rate is equal on both sides of every load. Return path is the same colour. |
| M2 | **Single-wire / sink model**: electricity flows *to* a device; no loop is needed. | Flowing dots along a route to a socket *before* the circuit closes (`src/game.ts:100`). A one-strand cable with no return shown. | Flowing dots only when the loop is closed. Path hints use a different look (see F2). |
| M3 | **The battery is a constant-current source**: it always pushes the same current. | A supply card that states current instead of volts. Fixed bead speed regardless of load. | Supplies show **V** (arcade shows "5 V" ✓). Current changes with the chain (arcade tested ✓). |
| M4 | **Sequential / local reasoning**: the first part "gets the current first"; changing a part downstream doesn't matter. | Animations that start current at + and creep around the loop. Placing a resistor "after" the LED appearing to do something different. | Current starts everywhere in the loop at once. The arcade chain sits *before* the LED; check that the rules give the same current if it were after. |
| M5 | **An LED is a bulb**: no resistor needed, polarity doesn't matter, brighter means more voltage. | An LED lit without a limit. Brightness tied to V. | Arcade: across-LED trap ✓, polarity flip ✓, light meter tied to I ✓. |
| M6 | **Short circuit = "a circuit that doesn't work"**, or extra current going "to ground". | Fault toasts saying "shorted" without showing the low-resistance path. A ground symbol drawn as a drain. | Toasts name the path ("the block across the LED limits nothing", shot 04 ✓). Star ground (garage) presented as *shared return wire resistance*, not "ground eats noise". |
| M7 | **A capacitor lets DC through / is a battery / stores current.** | A capacitor that "powers" a load indefinitely. "Charge" bars that never decay. | Lunch capacitor discharges and current stops when charged. Garage text ✓ (`src/stations/garage/logic.ts:183`). |
| M8 | **A fuse or breaker makes the device safe to touch / protects people.** | A fuse framed as "safety for Pip". | Fuse framed as protecting the *wire and parts* (arcade PTC protects the chain ✓). |
| M9 | **Water + any cable = short.** | **Lunch puddle trips the breaker when an intact cable lies in it** (F1). | Shorts only at an exposed junction (splitter, plug) or a scorched cable. |
| M10 | **Radio waves are sound**; invisible EM bands have colours; different bands travel at different speeds. | Radio band drawn **red**, IR pink or amber (`src/stations/spectrum/logic.ts:48-52`, F3). Ribbon speed differs per band. | Only the visible band gets its real hue; others get one neutral "invisible" style. Same propagation speed for every band. |
| M11 | **Voltage flows; current is pushed by voltage "through" a part** (voltage as substance). | Labels like "voltage through". | Wording uses "across" for V and "through" for I (arcade "ACROSS" ✓). Grep the strings. |
| M12 | **Water-pipe analogy taken literally** (wider wire = more pressure, magnetism ignored). | Coil or EMI lessons explained as "pressure surges". | Observatory and Garage use field or coil wording, not water. |

## D. Findings in this game (evidence-backed)

### F1. P1 · teaching: an intact cable in a puddle "shorts" (misconception M9)
- **Where:** `docs/LEVEL_02_LUNCH_RUSH.md:19` ("Cable in water = short → breaker trips"); `src/lunch-runtime.ts:207-208` (`wet()` is true whenever any cable segment crosses a puddle without a bridge); `src/sim/electrical.ts:23` (a wet lead trips its source as a `short`).
- **What's wrong:** an insulated, undamaged extension cord lying in shallow water does not short. Danger comes from exposed conductors: a plug, splitter or socket in the water, or damaged insulation. The game teaches that a cable merely *touching* water trips the breaker.
- **Why it matters:** AGENTS rule 10 ("truthful at the level taught"). Research shows short circuits are already poorly understood (Gardon et al.: 69% of pre-service teachers could not explain one). A wrong cause-and-effect rule teaches superstition, not safety. It also weakens the lesson: nothing tells the player what is actually dangerous.
- **Fix:** in `src/lunch-runtime.ts:208`, test only the **cable's junctions** (plug ends, any splitter, the reel) and any cable already marked scorched (`heat`/`dead` in `src/sim/electrical.ts`) against the puddles, not every segment. Give junctions a visible spark-and-steam effect when they sit in water. Change the doc line to "A plug or splitter in water = short". Keep the bridge and mop as fixes. Optionally add a frayed section to one lunch cable so the "damaged cable in water" case is taught explicitly.
- **Verify:** update the `tests/lunch-rules.spec.ts` case so that a cable crossing the puddle with both ends dry stays live, and a splitter placed in the puddle trips. Retake a lunch shot of each.

### F2. P1 · teaching/clarity: the route hint uses the "current flowing" look before any loop exists (M2)
- **Where:** `src/game.ts:100` (idle hint: 40 glowing cyan dots `#9ff3ea × 1.4` "flowing along the clean route to the socket"). The garage and qfn stations use flowing beads to mean **current** (`grep -l bead src` → `src/stations/garage/station.ts`, `src/stations/qfn/station.ts`).
- **What's wrong:** the same visual language, bright dots moving along a line, means "the path to take" in rooms and "current in a closed loop" at benches. In rooms the dots flow from the plug's position to the socket *while the circuit is open*. That reads exactly like the sink model: electricity runs to the device along one wire.
- **Why it matters:** this breaks R-TEACH-4. PhET's research says representations of invisible phenomena teach implicitly, so they teach misconceptions just as implicitly.
- **Fix:** restyle the idle hint as **static footprints or chevrons on the floor** (non-emissive, the `#ffd84a` warm yellow already used for the grab cue at `src/game.ts:96`), pulsing in place rather than travelling. Reserve flowing cyan beads for live, closed circuits only, and add them to the room cable when the machine runs, moving at the same speed on both conductors.
- **Verify:** take a meeting screenshot after 10 s idle (the hint shows). It must show no moving emissive dots. A second screenshot with the projector running shows beads on the cable.

### F3. P2 · teaching: invisible EM bands are drawn in visible colours (M10)
- **Where:** `src/stations/spectrum/logic.ts:48-52` (radio `#e5484d` red, microwave `#f08a4b` orange, thermal IR `#d9822b` amber, near-IR `#b0406a` pink, visible `#3fae6a` green); `src/stations/spectrum/station.ts:122,259` (the wave ribbon takes the band colour); `src/stations/observatory/room.ts:32-38` (the same colour family). Screenshot `research-external-10-spectrum-bench.jpg`: band keys RADIO, MICRO, THERMAL, NEAR IR and VISIBLE are coloured red, orange, orange, pink and green.
- **What's wrong:** giving radio "red" and near-IR "pink" suggests these waves have a colour. Students already confuse UV and IR (Libarkin 2011) and believe radio is sound or "different from light" (Tabor-Morris). The ribbon is already labelled "not to scale" ✓ but nothing says the colour is false.
- **Fix:** only the VISIBLE key and ribbon use a real hue (530 nm green ✓). Draw the four invisible bands in one neutral style: cool grey `#9aa0ad` with a small eye-slash icon on the key. Tell them apart by ribbon wavelength (already done: `cycles` 1 to 9) and by the key's label and icon (antenna, router, heat, remote). If colour must stay for usability, add "false colour" next to "ribbon not to scale" at `station.ts:327`. Do the same in Observatory.
- **Verify:** retake `research-external-10-spectrum-bench` with each band selected. Only Visible shows a hue; the monitor text includes "false colour" or the keys are neutral.

### F4. P2 · art-style/visual-bug: the garage kickback arc whites out the switch it is about
- **Where:** screenshot `research-external-08-garage-kickback-fail.jpg`, about 1 s after PULSE. The ACROSS SWITCH slot and the switch itself turn into a white bloom blob about 120×80 px; the switch silhouette and slot outline are gone. Compare `research-external-07-garage-bench.jpg`, where the switch reads clearly.
- **What's wrong / why it matters:** the arc *is* the lesson (inductive kickback at the switch node), but the bloom hides *where* it happens. This breaks AGENTS rule 9 ("restrained glow") and R-MOD-5. On an isometric bench, bloom spreads across the neighbouring labels.
- **Fix:** in the garage station's arc effect (`src/stations/garage/station.ts`, the arc/flash material), clamp emissive intensity so the arc stays under the bloom threshold, or exclude the flash quad from bloom. Draw the arc as a thin zigzag line with a 1–2 px dark outline, keep the switch body visible with toon shading through the event, and cap the flash to 120 ms. Keep the spark particles.
- **Verify:** retake shot 08 at +0.1, +0.4 and +1.0 s after `act('pulse')`. The switch's outline and the "ACROSS SWITCH" label stay legible in every frame.

### F5. P1 · clarity: benches carry too many words and show some values twice
- **Where:** `research-external-03-arcade-bench.jpg`. Around 45 words in the HUD alone: an objective card that repeats the title; a right card with the title, a two-line story ("The last tech clipped a block across the LED. The fuse keeps clicking.") and three telemetry rows; plus the prompt. The monitor separately shows CHAIN 0 Ω, CURRENT, LIGHT, LED HEAT, BLOCK HEAT. "Red LED on 5 V: 12–20 mA" appears **twice** (left card and right card). Chain appears **twice** (monitor "0 Ω", card "wire only"). Garage `research-external-08` also repeats values: the right card shows "Stop spike 77 V", the monitor shows "PEAK 77 V", and the toast says "spiked to 77 V". That's three copies.
- **Why it matters:** AGENTS rule 2 ("play by doing, not reading"). The seductive-details and redundancy research says duplicated text and flavour beside the lesson lower learning. The player's eye splits across three panels at once.
- **Fix:** while seated at a bench, collapse the left objective card to its icon and pip row (the job title is already on the right card). Remove right-card rows that duplicate a monitor value (arcade Chain/Current; garage Stop spike/Start sag), keeping only **Parts / credits**. Show the story line for 4 s on job start, then fade it. Toasts state the cause without repeating the number already on the scope. The files are the station panels in `src/stations/*/station.ts` and the objective card in `src/ui`.
- **Verify:** retake shots 03 and 08 and count words outside the in-world monitor. The target is 25 or fewer, and no value appears twice.

### F6. P2 · clarity: the arcade power key flips state during a fuse trip
- **Where:** `research-external-04-arcade-fuse-trip.jpg` (prompt "P · Fuse cooling") → `research-external-05-arcade-pinball-ok.jpg`. After fixing the chain, the natural retry (pressing P / `act('power')` again) turned the bench **off** ("POWER OFF"), and the prompt changed to "Power on". The spec's intended path avoids this by *not* pressing power again and waiting for the PTC fuse to recover (`tests/station-arcade.spec.ts:28`).
- **Why it matters:** the prompt binds P to a non-action. The player's instinct to "try again" is penalised by needing a second press, and nothing on screen says the PTC re-closes by itself, which is the lesson.
- **Fix:** while tripped, the P prompt reads "Power off". Or better, P is inert and the prompt shows a cooling ring icon with no key. Show the fuse visibly cooling from red to amber to normal. When it re-closes with a good chain, light the LED with no input. In `src/stations/arcade/station.ts`, ignore `power` toggles while `tripped`.
- **Verify:** script power → trip → fix → power. The bench ends **live** (`snap().station.live === true`) and the screenshot shows the LED lit.

### Positives confirmed (keep; don't regress)
- Failure toasts explain the physical cause ("The block across the LED limits nothing…", shot 04; "the switch node spiked to 77 V…", shot 08), each paired with an in-world cue (glowing fuse; scope trace with the 50 V reset line). This meets R-TEACH-2.
- Arcade uses **across** and **chain** wording and shows supply as a voltage (5 V), which guards against M3 and M11.
- The garage capacitor wording correctly says the cap gives the coil's current no path (`src/stations/garage/logic.ts:183`), which guards against M7.
- The Tab survey (`research-external-02-arcade-survey`) frames the whole room with the bench and storeroom readable, which answers Good Job!'s #1 complaint.
- The meeting start (`research-external-11`) shows the hero ring, the goal marker (yellow chevron) and a highlighted grab target. It passes R-CLAR-5 by eye.
