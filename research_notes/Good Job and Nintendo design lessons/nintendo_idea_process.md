## 1. Gameplay-first process: start from the verb, prototype with simple shapes, "upend the tea table", cut ideas

### Takeaway
Across many games, Nintendo teams first build a bare prototype of a single mechanic, often with placeholder shapes, and only build content once that mechanic feels good. Examples are Mario running in a block room (Mario 64), a sphere with gravity (Galaxy), fighting tofu cubes (Splatoon) and a top-down physics/chemistry sandbox (Breath of the Wild). Ideas are then kept or changed based on how they play, and late "tea-table upending" changes of direction are a known and accepted part of the culture.

### Cited Findings
- **Super Mario 64: movement came before levels.** The team first built a program on a powerful workstation with "a room made of simple Lego-like blocks" where Mario and Luigi could run, climb slopes and jump. They worked on analogue-stick control, and once it "felt smooth" they considered themselves "halfway there". This comes from the 1996 developer interviews (translated). — [shmuplations, Super Mario 64 1996 interviews](https://shmuplations.com/mario64/); summary in [daverupert.com](https://daverupert.com/2016/09/super-mario-64-1996-developer-interviews/)
- Mario 64 had no real controllers for about six months. Early Mario was driven by keyboard or modified Sega pads on the emulator boards. — [shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/mario64/) (search extract; reads like a Giles Goddard recollection, see [pixelatron Goddard interview](https://pixelatron.com/blog/the-making-of-super-mario-64-full-giles-goddard-interview-ngc/))
- **Levels as a clay diorama.** Miyamoto described level design as sculpting a diorama out of clay. They first made a very rough shape, put Mario in the map, adjusted it based on how he moved, then added ideas and changed the map step by step. He called the levels "garden dioramas". — [shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/mario64/)
- Early in Mario 64, Mario and the rabbit (MIPS) were the only characters. The team used them over and over for tests, for example having the rabbit follow Mario up a mountain while changing the camera viewpoint. — [shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/mario64/)
- A Mario 64 idea changed because of feel: chasing a very fast rabbit was "too harsh and stressful", so the race became one the player could lose to a Koopa. An animation of Mario tossing the rabbit by its ears was cut when time ran out. — [shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/mario64/); [GIGAZINE summary](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20160926-super-mario-64-interview/)
- **Super Mario Galaxy: the sphere prototype.** A small team spent about three months on a prototype: spherical terrain placed in space with gravity added. It looked like "a bare minimum version" of the final game, and it is where "development really took off". — [Iwata Asks: Super Mario Galaxy, Vol. 2 "Benefits of a Spherical Field"](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-Super-Mario-Galaxy/Volume-2-The-Developers/2-Benefits-of-a-Spherical-Field/2-Benefits-of-a-Spherical-Field-222607.html); [Iwata Asks: How Super Mario Galaxy Was Born](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/super_mario_galaxy/0/0/)
- **Splatoon: tofu cubes.** The first demo, made by program director Sato, was "a white cube in a maze". Black and white tofu blocks shot ink at each other to claim turf, with a map on the TV and a 3D view on the GamePad. When a tofu slid over ink of its own colour it blended in and could not be seen. That unplanned "hiding" discovery became the squid that dives into ink. — [Iwata Asks: Splatoon, "1. It Started With Tofu"](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-Splatoon/Splatoon/1-It-Started-With-Tofu/1-It-Started-With-Tofu-1021365.html); [Iwata Asks Splatoon p.1](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wiiu/splatoon/0/0/); secondary: [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2015/05/splatoons_iwata_asks_interview_considers_its_origins_of_tofu_rabbits_and_more)
- The Splatoon interview also covers character ideas the team tried and dropped, including rabbits, before settling on squid. (Headline-level detail only; the full text was not fetched.) — [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2015/05/splatoons_iwata_asks_interview_considers_its_origins_of_tofu_rabbits_and_more)
- **Breath of the Wild: a physics prototype in 2D.** Director Hidemaro Fujibayashi asked technical director Takuhiro Dohta to build physics and "chemistry" into an NES-style top-down prototype. It was really a top-down view of a 3D world, which let the team test the real systems. The chemistry engine is a rule-based state calculator: "elements" (fire, water, ice) act on "materials" (trees, rocks, the player). The team called the approach "multiplicative gameplay". Presented at GDC 2017. — [Zelda Universe GDC report](https://zeldauniverse.net/2017/03/09/zelda-team-discusses-breath-of-the-wilds-development-and-2d-zelda-prototype-at-gdc/); [Thumbsticks GDC report](https://www.thumbsticks.com/gdc-17-breath-of-the-wild-science-lies/); [Bleeding Cool](https://bleedingcool.com/games/gdc-nintendos-2d-breath-wild-prototype-actually-3d/)
- **"Upending the tea table" (chabudai gaeshi).** The phrase comes from the manga *Star of the Giants*, in which a father flips the dinner table. At Nintendo it means someone, usually Miyamoto, stepping in late in development to change the game's direction sharply. — [Zelda Dungeon Wiki](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/wiki/Upending_the_Tea_Table) (secondary); primary examples: [Iwata Asks: Luigi's Mansion 2, "3. Upending the Tea Table"](https://www.nintendo.com/en-za/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-Luigi-s-Mansion-2/Luigi-s-Mansion-2/3-Upending-the-Tea-Table/3-Upending-the-Tea-Table-741981.html); [Iwata Asks: Ocarina of Time 3D, "Koji Kondo Upends the Tea Table"](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-The-Legend-of-Zelda-Ocarina-of-Time-3D/Vol-1-Sound/2-Koji-Kondo-Upends-the-Tea-Table/2-Koji-Kondo-Upends-the-Tea-Table-231296.html)
- In Luigi's Mansion 2, one such change turned the level architecture into the challenge: the stairs themselves became the "boss" of one building and a clock became the boss of another. — [Iwata Asks: Luigi's Mansion 2](https://www.nintendo.com/en-za/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-Luigi-s-Mansion-2/Luigi-s-Mansion-2/3-Upending-the-Tea-Table/3-Upending-the-Tea-Table-741981.html) (via search extract)
- For Super Mario 3D World, Miyamoto said there was "no 'upending the tea table' this time. Which is unusual." The interview notes that "Everything you made went in". This shows that throwing work away is the normal case. — [Iwata Asks: Super Mario 3D World](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wiiu/super-mario-3d-world/0/0/); summary: [GoNintendo](https://gonintendo.com/?amp=&id=216962&mode=viewstory)
- Miyamoto later said he has "moved on" from upending the tea table mid-development. — [Nintendo Everything](https://nintendoeverything.com/miyamoto-on-his-work-at-nintendo-these-days-upending-the-tea-table-how-he-works-with-staff/) (headline only; not read)
- **Miyamoto's creed:** "A good idea is something that does not solve just one single problem, but rather can solve multiple problems at once." Attributed to the 1991 *Nintendo Mario Mania Player's Guide*. — [Wikiquote](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Shigeru_Miyamoto) (the quote is widely repeated; the original page was not checked)
- **Pikmin's origin** was Miyamoto's gardening hobby and watching ants carry leaves, together with a long-held wish to make a "managing things" game. — [Iwata Asks, Wii Fit Vol. 1 (referenced)](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/wii_fit/0/0/); secondary: [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/pikmin-has-the-best-origin-story-1657571859), [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2013/07/feature_a_brief_history_of_pikmin)

### Inferences
- For Circuit Crew, the order would be: (1) a grey-box room with the player character and one grabbable, snappable wire or component, and nothing else, (2) tune grab, carry, drop and snap until they feel good, (3) only then design puzzles. The rooms are "clay dioramas" shaped around how the character actually moves.
- The Splatoon "hiding in ink" and BotW "chemistry" stories suggest letting the physics sandbox surprise you: record emergent behaviour in prototypes and promote the best of it into mechanics. Electricity has natural "elements act on materials" rules (conductive, insulating, wet, overloaded) that fit a BotW-style rule table.
- The tea-table habit means budgeting for late changes of direction and treating discarded work as normal.

### Gaps
- No primary Nintendo source was found for the exact phrase "if it's not fun in a grey box, it won't be". It appears to be industry folklore rather than a Nintendo quote, and should not be attributed to Nintendo.
- The "Mario 64 castle grounds as a garden for touching things" anecdote was not found in a primary source. Only the "garden diorama" phrasing (shmuplations) was confirmed.
- Full, verbatim Iwata Asks texts could not be fetched (egress blocked).

## 2. Iwata Asks lessons on finding the fun and playtesting

### Takeaway
Nintendo tests constantly and informally. Directors play the build every day, anyone can pitch, and the target is a visible player reaction such as laughter or "this is ridiculous!", not a checklist.

### Cited Findings
- On Super Mario Bros. Wonder, Koichi Hayashida played the prototype every day in different ways. He found surprises, including sounds and visuals he had not known were in the game. — [Ask the Developer Vol. 11, Super Mario Bros. Wonder, Chapter 2](https://www.nintendo.com/au/news-and-articles/ask-the-developer-vol-11-super-mario-bros-wonder-chapter-2/)
- WarioWare: Smooth Moves: the team's goal was to get Iwata to say "This is ridiculous!" or "The game beat me!" with a grin. Iwata said this while spinning a chair around during testing, and the team came to see it as the highest compliment. — [Iwata Asks: WarioWare Smooth Moves, "2. 'This is ridiculous' is the best compliment"](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-Wii/Iwata-Asks-WarioWare-Smooth-Moves/2-This-is-ridiculous-is-the-best-compliment/2-This-is-ridiculous-is-the-best-compliment-228514.html)
- Galaxy's lead programmer only played the game from the start near the end of development, partly to debug. He then thought, "I'm playing a game like nothing that's come before it". The takeaway is that playing the whole flow, not isolated parts, is what reveals the experience. — [Iwata Asks: Super Mario Galaxy Vol. 2](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/super_mario_galaxy/1/1/)
- Nintendo Labo: the first prototype brainstorm ran about three weeks, and the team "pushed aside" many prototypes because they were not fun. — [Nintendo Labo developer interview, Part 2: Prototyping and Development](https://www.nintendo.com/au/news-and-articles/nintendo-labo-developer-interview-part-2-prototyping-and-development/) (via search extract)
- Super Mario Bros. World 1-1: Miyamoto and Tezuka "kept simulating what the player would do". This is imagined playtesting, used to shape the first screen. — [Game Developer, "How Miyamoto built Super Mario Bros.' legendary World 1-1"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-miyamoto-built-i-super-mario-bros-i-legendary-world-1-1) (reporting a 2015 Eurogamer video interview)

### Inferences
- Use a reaction-based fun test: in playtests, record laughs, "whoa" moments and spontaneous retries, not just completion. A room that produces no reaction is a candidate to cut or rework.
- Play the whole build from the start regularly, not just the room you are working on.

### Gaps
- No detailed primary source was found on Nintendo's formal QA or external-playtester pipeline (for example, the "Mario Club" debug/test team). That would need another search.

## 3. WarioWare, Labo, Animal Crossing, Mario Maker, Splatoon: layering ideas, variety, surprise, humour

### Takeaway
Nintendo gets variety from an open ideas pool where anyone can pitch, with cheap filters on what gets built (buildability, fun in a prototype). It adds humour and surprise on top of a stable core. Labo's "Make, Play, Discover" gives equal weight to discovering how the thing works inside.

### Cited Findings
- In WarioWare, anyone can pitch, including people outside the team, as a storyboard. Programmers then pick ideas that look easy to make and build them. — [Iwata Asks: WarioWare D.I.Y., "4. Sharing New Microgames"](https://www.nintendo.com/en-za/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-WarioWare-D-I-Y-/Iwata-Asks-WarioWare-D-I-Y-/4-Sharing-New-Microgames/4-Sharing-New-Microgames-215173.html); [Iwata Asks WarioWare D.I.Y. p.4](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/ds/diy/0/3/)
- A whole WarioWare microgame pitch could be one picture and one word. The nose-picking microgame's plan was a drawing of a nose with a finger beside it and the word "Pick!". — same sources as above (search extract)
- The Labo slogan is "Make, Play, Discover". The developers stressed that "Play and Discover are just as important as the Make aspect", and that cardboard let users build, fix and change things themselves. — [Nintendo Labo developer interview, Part 1: The Concept](https://www.nintendo.com/au/news-and-articles/nintendo-labo-developer-interview-part-1-the-concept/); [Part 2](https://www.nintendo.com/au/news-and-articles/nintendo-labo-developer-interview-part-2-prototyping-and-development/)
- Labo's hardware team joined after the software team showed them a "Carry-Con" prototype. The designer of the Switch stand later designed the Toy-Con Piano. The reveal video deliberately showed the piano's lid opening onto an empty inside, because "there's something really interesting about all that sound coming out of an empty Toy-Con". Part 2 also mentions a "nose-picking prototype". — [Labo interview Part 2](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/News/2018/April/Nintendo-Labo-developer-interview-Part-2-Prototyping-and-Development-1369593.html); [GoNintendo summary](https://gonintendo.com/stories/307407-nintendo-labo-dev-interview-part-2-a-nose-picking-prototype-d)
- Labo's Discover mode explains how each Toy-Con works (IR camera, reflective tape and so on). — [Nintendo Labo official developer interview page](https://labo.nintendo.com/ca/developer-interview/) (not fetched; mentioned in search listing)

### Inferences
- The Labo "open the lid and it's empty" moment fits Circuit Crew directly: let players open panels and see the electricity flow, turning the explanation into a toy ("Discover") instead of text.
- A WarioWare-style one-picture, one-word pitch format works well for a small team's idea backlog of room gimmicks.

### Gaps
- No primary sources were gathered on Animal Crossing or Mario Maker ideation within this call budget.

## 4. Game feel and feedback: juice, sound, reactions, "touching is fun", physics humour

### Takeaway
Nintendo's primary sources point to feel as the foundation (smooth 3D movement came first in Mario 64) and to reactive audio/visual surprises that reward poking around (Hayashida finding sounds and visuals in daily play of Wonder). Physics humour comes from systems reacting believably (BotW chemistry) and from absurd transformations (Wonder effects).

### Cited Findings
- Mario 64's foundation was controls that felt "nice and responsive". Once those felt smooth, the team felt "halfway there". — [shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/mario64/)
- Wonder's prototypes were full of sounds and visuals that surprised even the director in daily play. — [Ask the Developer Vol. 11, Chapter 2](https://www.nintendo.com/au/news-and-articles/ask-the-developer-vol-11-super-mario-bros-wonder-chapter-2/)
- Wonder effects turn Mario into a Goomba, start a stampede that carries him past the level's end, or puff him up like a balloon. These are comedic transformations. — [Wikipedia: Super Mario Bros. Wonder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_Bros._Wonder) (secondary; cites the dev interviews)
- Wonder's Talking Flowers make short comments in levels. Game Developer called them "Nintendo's very own Mr Motivator". — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-talking-flowers-in-super-mario-bros-wonder-became-nintendo-s-very-own-mr-motivator) (headline only)
- A rejected Wonder idea: a life-sized, "eight-heads-tall" live-action Mario humming along to the music and saying "boing!" when jumping. It shows the team welcomed absurd humour even when cutting it. — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/nintendo-switch/super-mario-bros-wonder-devs-had-over-2000-ideas-for-the-game-including-the-addition-of-an-enormous-live-action-mario-that-didnt-make-the-cut), citing [Ask the Developer Vol. 11, Part 1](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-11-super-mario-bros-wonder-part-1/)
- BotW's physics and chemistry let players "push an innocuous boulder all the way to the final boss". It is a high degree of freedom that comes from consistent rules. — [Zelda Universe GDC 2017](https://zeldauniverse.net/2017/03/09/zelda-team-discusses-breath-of-the-wilds-development-and-2d-zelda-prototype-at-gdc/)

### Inferences
- For Circuit Crew, every touchable object should react (sound, squash, spark, character comment). Players should find reactions by poking around, the way Hayashida did.
- Comic failure states (a short circuit that frizzes the character's hair or blows the room's lights) turn failure into a reward, in the same spirit as Wonder's transformations.

### Gaps
- No Nintendo primary source was found that uses the word "juice" (a Western indie term, from Jonasson/Purho's 2012 "Juice it or lose it" talk). Do not attribute it to Nintendo.
- No primary quote was found for the "Miyamoto garden / touching is fun" framing of Mario 64's castle grounds. It is unverified.

## 5. Minimal text UI: conveying goals with icons and in-world cues

### Takeaway
Nintendo's clearest primary example is World 1-1. The first screen teaches the rules through level layout and enemy choice instead of text, and the team changed content (a Koopa became a Goomba) when the lesson did not read clearly.

### Cited Findings
- In a 2015 Eurogamer interview, Miyamoto and Tezuka said World 1-1 was built to teach, without instructions, how to avoid enemies, how to defeat them, how ? blocks work, and how to tell a Goomba from a mushroom. They "kept simulating what the player would do" so that "even within that one section, the player would understand the general concept of what Mario is supposed to be and what the game is about." — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-miyamoto-built-i-super-mario-bros-i-legendary-world-1-1)
- The first enemy was originally going to be a Koopa Troopa. Teaching its jump-and-kick at the very start did not work well, so they created the simpler, stompable Goomba. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-miyamoto-built-i-super-mario-bros-i-legendary-world-1-1); [Wikipedia: World 1-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_1-1)
- WarioWare microgames give instructions as a single verb ("Pick!") plus the picture itself. — [Iwata Asks: WarioWare D.I.Y.](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/ds/diy/0/3/)
- In Wonder, the game plays differently depending on whether you touch the Wonder Flower. The flower is a clear in-world object, and every course had to be designed with both states. — [Wikipedia: Super Mario Bros. Wonder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_Bros._Wonder) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Circuit Crew's first room should be a 1-1: a dead lamp, a visible break in the wire and one component lying nearby. Physically, the break should look like the "Goomba" (the simplest thing to fix), and harder components should come later.
- When a room needs any text at all, limit it to one verb per room (WarioWare-style "Connect!") plus an icon.

### Gaps
- No Nintendo primary interview was found here specifically on HUD/icon design (for example, Splatoon's ink-colour map or Zelda's icon language). A dedicated search would be needed.

## 6. Kirby / Mario Wonder: surprise and the 2,000 sticky notes

### Takeaway
Wonder's process was divergent, then convergent. First everyone (programmers, designers, sound staff) wrote ideas on sticky notes with no conditions, about 2,000 of them. Then regular "Wonder prototype meetings" picked ideas that fit and prototyped them. The team was allowed to put content ahead of schedule, and the director's goal was to bring back the original game's "secrets and mysteries".

### Cited Findings
- Hayashida wanted to bring 3D-Mario-style surprises into 2D, so the team held an idea-sharing session. Everyone was asked to write "any and all ideas" on sticky notes "without conditions", because conditions at the start would stifle creativity. Hayashida counted more than 2,000 notes. — [Ask the Developer Vol. 11, Part 1](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-11-super-mario-bros-wonder-part-1/); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/nintendo-switch/super-mario-bros-wonder-devs-had-over-2000-ideas-for-the-game-including-the-addition-of-an-enormous-live-action-mario-that-didnt-make-the-cut)
- Next came "Wonder prototype meetings" that chose ideas from the 2,000+ notes and built prototypes matching the world of Wonder. — [Ask the Developer Vol. 11, Chapter 2](https://www.nintendo.com/au/news-and-articles/ask-the-developer-vol-11-super-mario-bros-wonder-chapter-2/)
- The team was allowed "to prioritize content over schedule". — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/super-mario-bros-wonder-devs-were-allowed-to-prioritize-content-over-schedule-so-they-ended-up-with-2000-sticky-notes-of-inspiration/) (headline wording; full text not fetched)
- Director Shiro Mouri made "secrets and mysteries" a core concept. He wanted to recreate the original Super Mario Bros.' sense of mystery for a modern audience and to push back against a "standardized idea of mysteries and secrets". — [Wikipedia: Super Mario Bros. Wonder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_Bros._Wonder); [EssentiallySports](https://www.essentiallysports.com/esports-news-how-super-mario-bros-wonder-combats-the-standardized-idea-of-mysteries-and-secrets-of-the-franchise-director-shiro-mouri-explains/) (secondary)
- Mouri consulted Hayashida, who had worked on Super Mario Odyssey, about how Odyssey built in surprises. The result was described as a "toy box of ideas". — [Ask the Developer Vol. 11, Chapter 2](https://www.nintendo.com/au/news-and-articles/ask-the-developer-vol-11-super-mario-bros-wonder-chapter-2/)
- Sources disagree on what the ~2,000 ideas were. Some say general gameplay ideas (TechRadar / Ask the Developer); others say Wonder Flower effects specifically (Wikipedia, KitGuru: "2000 different effects"). — [KitGuru](https://www.kitguru.net/tech-news/mustafa-mahmoud/super-mario-bros-wonder-originally-had-over-2000-different-effects/) vs. [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/nintendo-switch/super-mario-bros-wonder-devs-had-over-2000-ideas-for-the-game-including-the-addition-of-an-enormous-live-action-mario-that-didnt-make-the-cut). The primary interview wording seems to be "gameplay ideas" collected from everyone.
- One Wonder Flower per main level; its effect always leads to a Wonder Seed. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_Bros._Wonder)

### Inferences
- Copyable ritual: (1) a timed, no-rules sticky-note session in which every discipline, including sound, writes room gimmicks, (2) a weekly "prototype meeting" that picks 2 or 3 notes to grey-box, (3) one "Wonder moment" per room: a hidden switch or component that changes the room's rules for a while (gravity flips, wires come alive, the lights become a disco). It should also lead to a collectible, so surprise is always rewarded.

### Gaps
- No Kirby-specific (HAL) primary sources on surprise or ideation were gathered.
- The exact "secrets and mysteries" wording should be checked in the Ask the Developer text, which could not be fetched.
