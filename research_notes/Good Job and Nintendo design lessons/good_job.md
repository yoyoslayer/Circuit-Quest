## Level structure: floors, stages, tasks, size

### Takeaway
There are 9 floors, and each is a company department. Floors 1–7 have 4 stages each, and the last two floors are different, for about 32 stages in total. Each stage has one mostly unique office task (for example: run the internet cable, set up the projector, gather the workers, clean up goo, deliver packages, mop). A stage takes 5–30 minutes. A few stages are noticeably larger than the rest, and reviewers disliked that.

### Cited Findings
- Good Job! is a 2020 puzzle game from Dutch developer Paladin Studios, published by Nintendo for Switch. You play the CEO's son, who completes "exaggerated office tasks in destructive playground-style puzzles to climb the corporate ladder." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Job!)
- "Each of the nine floors of the office tower represents a different facet of the business, such as maintenance, finance, or marketing, and the tasks on that floor are generally aligned with its role in the company." — [Nintendo World Report](http://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/53370/good-job-switch-review)
- "Except for the final two floors, the first seven each contain four stages, and by completing all four you earn a promotion that unlocks the next floor. This makes for a total of 32 stages, most of which have unique objectives." — [Nintendo World Report](http://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/53370/good-job-switch-review)
- Official store copy: "Your job duties can vary from fixing the office's internet connection to cleaning up experimental goo. Complete puzzles in multiple ways as you experiment with the interactive environments—a projector can be wheeled gently through an electric door…or catapulted through the wall." — [Nintendo.com store page](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/good-job-switch/) (surfaced via search; the page itself was not fetched)
- Floor 1 stage names: "Set up the Projector", "Reroute the Internet Cable", "Gather the Workers" — [YouTube walkthrough title](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IV-AYHR2PkU)
- Other example tasks: "delivering packages and mopping the floor" — search summary citing [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/2020/04/good-job-review) / [Nintendo Enthusiast](https://www.nintendoenthusiast.com/good-job-review-for-nintendo-switch-making-work-fun/) (the summary did not say which of the two)
- Forklift stages: "some puzzles see you driving forklifts to pick up packages that are well out of reach"; "in one early level, you need to reconnect the Internet cable from one end of the office to the other" — [Eggplante](https://www.eggplante.com/2020/04/16/good-job-review/)
- Each of the nine floors offers "a genuinely unique series of challenges and an incredibly varied set of possible solutions for each task." — [Eggplante](https://www.eggplante.com/2020/04/16/good-job-review/)
- Size: "There are a few levels that are much larger in size than the rest, which makes navigating them at your walking pace a bit of a chore." — [GameSpot (7/10)](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/good-job-review-office-space/1900-6417443/)
- Length: about 32 levels; a stage can take 20–30 minutes at first, or 5–8 minutes with practice. Players report 4–5 hours to finish and about 20 hours for 100% completion. — [GameFAQs Q&A](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/switch/286598-good-job/answers/542469-how-long-is-this-game) / [Nintendo Life forum](https://www.nintendolife.com/forums/nintendo-switch/good_job-_how_good_really_is_it) (user-reported, not official)
- GameSpot called the playtime "brief" but "well-balanced" by variety. — [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/good-job-review-office-space/1900-6417443/)

### Inferences
- Structure to copy: **themed floor → 4 short task stages → promotion.** Each floor theme sets the verbs for its tasks (a maintenance floor gets cabling and cleaning). For Circuit Crew, this could be facility wings (power, server room, lab, and so on) with 3–4 circuit jobs each.
- Tuning target: a first run of about 5–30 minutes per stage, and 5–8 minutes for a practiced run. Keep most stages one screen or a few connected rooms, because the stages reviewers called oversized were the ones they found tedious.
- Almost every stage has a *different* objective, and reviewers named this variety as the main thing holding a short game together.

## Room density: interactable and physics objects per room

### Takeaway
Reviewers describe levels as crowded with breakable office props: light fixtures, statues, chairs, walls, coffee mugs, trophies, glass windows, cabinets. One review summary says "hundreds" per level. I could not check any object count against screenshots.

- Play involves "breaking holes in the wall, dropping shiny trophies on the ground, and causing utter destruction." — same search summary as above (outlet unconfirmed)
- Walls are breakable: the store copy says a projector can be "catapulted through the wall." — [Nintendo.com](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/good-job-switch/)
- Props that reviewers mention being launched: cabinets and chairs, sent flying by a power-cord slingshot — [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/good_job); workers thrown through glass windows — [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/2020/04/good-job-review)
- Some levels ask you to move "around thirty items one-by-one," which one reviewer found "repetitive to the point of being dull." — search summary covering [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2020/04/08/good-job-switch-review/), [SelectButton](https://selectbutton.com/reviews/good-job-review), [Eggplante](https://www.eggplante.com/2020/04/16/good-job-review/) and others (outlet not pinned down; a targeted search of TheSixthAxis did not confirm it)

### Inferences
- **ESTIMATE, not verified:** if "hundreds" per level is roughly right and a level has several rooms or zones, that works out to very roughly 30–80+ physics or breakable props per room. This is my own arithmetic from an unconfirmed claim, not a measurement.
- The props fall into three groups: (a) objective items (the projector, the cable, the packages), (b) obstacles that can also be used as tools (cabinets, chairs, walls, doors, glass), and (c) small breakables whose main job is juicy damage feedback (mugs, trophies, lights, statues). Circuit Crew could mirror this: generators and cable spools / racks, crates and doors / fuses, bulbs and monitors.
- If an objective needs about 30 identical carries, it reads as busywork. Batch those items (a cart or a forklift) or give fewer, more interesting items.

## Core mechanics: grab, pull and carry; cables and plugs; vehicles and tools; launching; destruction

### Takeaway
There is one interact/grab button plus the stick. The key physics toy is the **elastic power or internet cable**: you drag it around corners, it has to stretch far enough to reach, and when pulled tight it becomes a slingshot. Other tools are forklifts, wheeled items and the office furniture itself. Destruction is a normal, allowed shortcut.

### Cited Findings
- "Controls are simple -- move with the joystick, tap a button to interact with objects -- and players are given freedom to approach and solve problems as they see fit." — [Nintendo World Report (9/10)](http://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/53370/good-job-switch-review)
- Cables: "You might have to drag cables across the workplace floor, bending them around corners and ensuring they stretch far enough to reach." — search summary of reviews incl. [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2020/04/08/good-job-switch-review/) / [SelectButton](https://selectbutton.com/reviews/good-job-review) (outlet not pinned down)
- Cable slingshot: "using a power cord stretched taught across a hallway to create a slingshot-like band that can launch cabinets and chairs across the office." — [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/good_job)
- The internet cable stage can be solved with "creative solutions... like setting up an electrical cord slingshot." — [Eggplante](https://www.eggplante.com/2020/04/16/good-job-review/)
- Physics problems: "Objects can glitch through springy power cords"; "forklift trucks often have difficulty picking up objects, or those objects alternate between defying gravity while balanced on a fork or falling through when bumped"; "objects required to solve puzzles will get stuck between poles." Forklift levels were "full of awkward, takes-ages-to-correct situations with setting down crates in the wrong place." — search summary across [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2020/04/08/good-job-switch-review/), [SelectButton](https://selectbutton.com/reviews/good-job-review), [Switch Player](https://switchplayer.net/2020/04/18/good-job-review/), [Eggplante](https://www.eggplante.com/2020/04/16/good-job-review/) (outlet for each quote not pinned down)
- A projector can be "wheeled gently through an electric door…or catapulted through the wall." — [Nintendo.com](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/good-job-switch/)
- Local co-op: pass a Joy-Con to a second player to "help solve puzzles, divvy up tasks, or collect in-game outfits." — Nintendo store copy via search ([Nintendo.com](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/good-job-switch/))
- Nintendo Life: "some irritating controls and things having a tendency to find themselves stuck." — [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/good_job) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- For Circuit Crew, **the cable is the star mechanic**, and this is directly supported by the sources. A springy cable with limited length that wraps around corners is both the puzzle constraint (reach and routing) and the chaos toy (slingshot). Its tension should be readable and tunable.
- The biggest risks are precision vehicles (the forklift) and objects wedging in geometry. Give forgiving pickup and drop zones, snap assists for plugs, and anti-stuck nudges or respawns for objective items.
- A one-button interaction keeps it accessible. The depth comes from what objects *do*, not from how many inputs there are.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the full tool and vehicle list (other vehicles or tools, such as leaf blowers, tape or carts, are unconfirmed). I also could not confirm whether cables have a fixed max length or break when overstretched.

## Scoring: speed vs. mess and damage

### Takeaway
The game grades three things: time, number of objects destroyed, and repair cost in money. The overall grade follows the *best* of the three (in practice, time), so a fast, destructive run still earns a top grade. Being chaotic is rewarded rather than punished, and replaying for better grades is optional.

### Cited Findings
- "Upon completing a task, the player is graded on how long it took to complete the level, as well as the number of objects destroyed and the monetary repair costs for damage. Although grades are given for all three of these, the overall grade is generally based around time, so causing costly damage but completing the level quickly will still result in a good overall grade." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Job!)
- "You also earn an overall ranking that seems to just match your biggest grade, and so even if you destroy an entire office and earn two D's for destruction and an A for speed, your final grade will still be an A." — [Nintendo World Report](http://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/53370/good-job-switch-review) (search summary; a similar phrasing may come from TheSixthAxis)
- "True star employees can also replay stages to try to earn a higher score by maximizing speed while minimizing damages." — Nintendo store copy via search ([Nintendo.com](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/good-job-switch/))
- Tasks can be done "non-destructively" or chaotically. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Job!)
- "Its grading system means there are plenty of opportunities to replay." — [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/good_job)
- More than 100 collectible outfits, "from business casual to Viking chic," are hidden in the office puzzles and give another reason to replay. — Nintendo store copy via search ([Nintendo.com](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/good-job-switch/))

### Inferences
- The "max of the grades" rule is a deliberate way to avoid judging the player: chaos is never punished and carefulness is optional. Circuit Crew could keep separate Time / Damage / Parts-wasted letter grades but not let damage drag the headline grade down. Players who want a challenge can chase a "clean and fast" result.
- Showing the damage cost as a money total (a repair bill) is itself a punchline, because a big number reads as a joke rather than a penalty.

### Gaps
- I could not find the exact grade thresholds or the full letter scale (the sources confirm A and D exist), and I could not confirm whether an S rank exists.

## Humor and feedback

### Takeaway
The humor comes from physics slapstick against a mundane office backdrop: workers thrown through windows, projectiles fired from cables, expensive crystal-like shattering. Sound design and elevator-music-style tunes were praised. There is a wordless nepotism premise.

## UI, text and camera

### Takeaway
Communication is almost entirely wordless: pop-up icons, characters styled like warning-sign pictograms, a wordless intro, and no tutorials. The camera is fixed or automatic with no player control, which reviewers named as the main technical criticism. The lack of explanation sometimes confused players.

### Cited Findings
- The game uses "pop up icons" as its primary communication. "Humans are modeled after the icons used in warning signs." It has "no tutorials and no guidance or help when in the level." — search summary of [Gert Lush Gaming](https://gertlushgaming.co.uk/review-good-job-nintendo-switch/), [3rd-strike](https://3rd-strike.com/good-job-review/), [Use a Potion!](https://www.useapotion.com/2020/03/good-job-review/) (not pinned down)
- The first prototype had stick figures, and its buildings and objects were black and white. Nintendo liked the simplicity and asked Paladin to "keep it simple" but "make sure it won't look plain." — [Nintendo Everything interview](https://nintendoeverything.com/good-job-interview-nintendo-paladin-studios/) via [GoNintendo](https://www.gonintendo.com/stories/364413-nintendo-explains-how-their-collaboration-with-paladin-studios-fo) / [My Nintendo News](https://mynintendonews.com/2020/07/05/nintendo-and-paladin-studios-talk-about-their-relationship-on-good-job/)
- "Without direct camera control it's more challenging to survey larger levels, making them far less fun to play through." — [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/good-job-review-office-space/1900-6417443/)
- "Minor issues such as not being able to control the camera and occasionally finicky controls." — [Nintendo World Report](http://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/53370/good-job-switch-review)
- The game "doesn't do a great job of explaining how everything works." The reviewer got stuck "thinking the game was glitched when they just didn't understand the mechanics." — [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/2020/04/good-job-review)

### Inferences
- Circuit Crew should use icon-first task prompts (a plug icon for "power this," and so on) with very little text. It also needs clear affordance hints, such as highlighting what can be plugged in or grabbed, so players don't mistake missing knowledge for a bug. Letting the player rotate or zoom the camera, or at least adding auto-framing on large rooms, answers the most repeated criticism.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the exact camera angle (it appears to be high isometric or third-person, but I could not verify that without screenshots), or the HUD layout (whether there is a timer or a damage counter on screen).

## Critical reception

### Takeaway
Reviews were generally favorable: Metacritic 78, OpenCritic 80% recommend. The inventive physics, freedom in how to solve things, humor, charm and co-op were praised. The criticism: no camera control, finicky controls, physics wedging, clumsy forklift levels, repetitive carry-many-items levels, poor explanation of mechanics, oversized levels, and a "samey" feel after a few hours.

### Cited Findings
- Metacritic 78%, OpenCritic 80% recommend. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Job!); [Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/game/good-job/); [OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/game/9245/good-job-)
- Nintendo World Report 9/10: "Every floor of the office tower feels fresh." — [NWR](http://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/53370/good-job-switch-review)
- GameSpot 7/10: "finds a middle ground between being a destructive playground and an inventive puzzler." — [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/good-job-review-office-space/1900-6417443/)
- Nintendo Life: "2020's answer to Untitled Goose Game... a superb example of why excellent gameplay matters above all else." — [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/good_job)
- Twinfinite: surprises in "the first hour or two," then it "starts to feel bland and samey." — [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/reviews/good-job-review/)
- TheSixthAxis: "a few rough edges and occasional annoyances from the genre as a whole." — [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2020/04/08/good-job-switch-review/)
- Switch Player: "fun, addictive and really accessible"; co-op "adds to the fun." — [Switch Player](https://switchplayer.net/2020/04/18/good-job-review/)

### Inferences
- Lessons for Circuit Crew: (1) keep changing the objective every stage to fight "samey"; (2) avoid long fetch-quantity tasks; (3) make vehicles and precise placement forgiving; (4) give camera control; (5) teach each new object's behavior once, with a visual hint.

## Developer statements: design process, prototyping, physics

### Takeaway
Paladin's stated process: prototype a "main experience" verb (for example "watering plants") quickly to prove it is fun. Then build the level layout and the **non-destructive** solution first, because that is the hardest part of the level design, and only then add **destructive shortcuts** on top. Nintendo met Paladin in 2017 around a stick-figure, black-and-white prototype.

### Cited Findings
- Coen Neessen (game director): they would explore the "main experience" such as "watering plants" and quickly prototype mechanics and short scenarios to prove it was fun. Then they created the layout and the non-destructive puzzle, "the most difficult thing to get right level design wise," and then added destructive shortcuts on top. — [Nintendo Everything interview](https://nintendoeverything.com/good-job-interview-nintendo-paladin-studios/) (via search excerpt)
- The interviewees were Takao Nakano (Nintendo of America producer), Masataka Takemoto (Nintendo Co., Ltd. director), and Robert Abercrombie (lead producer) and Coen Neessen (game director) from Paladin. — [Nintendo Everything](https://nintendoeverything.com/good-job-interview-nintendo-paladin-studios/)
- Paladin founder Derk de Geus had tried to get in with Nintendo for about 10 years. A Nintendo team met Paladin in 2017 and was interested in one of their prototypes. — [GoNintendo](https://www.gonintendo.com/stories/364413-nintendo-explains-how-their-collaboration-with-paladin-studios-fo) / [My Nintendo News](https://mynintendonews.com/2020/07/05/nintendo-and-paladin-studios-talk-about-their-relationship-on-good-job/)
- Paladin's goals: accessible to a broad audience, enjoyable for children and adults, simple controls. They chose an office setting for recognizability. — same sources
- Technical designer Niels van Egmond worked with Neessen on Good Job! and is described as specializing in "early prototyping and refining game feel." — [Niels van Egmond portfolio](https://nielsvanegmond.github.io/)

### Inferences
- The most useful lesson for Circuit Crew: **design each room around a guaranteed clean solution first** (for example, route the cable properly through the doors), **then add the chaos shortcuts** (smash a wall, slingshot the generator). This guarantees solvability and makes the chaos feel like a clever discovery.
