# Build Clean Solutions First, Then Chaos

**Bottom line:** Good Job! and Nintendo teams tend to work the same way. They find one verb that feels good in a grey box. They build each small room around one idea and a guaranteed clean solution. Then they layer physics shortcuts and slapstick on top, and they teach through layout and icons instead of text. For Circuit Crew, the springy, length-limited cable is the star mechanic. The rooms that reviewers criticised in Good Job! point to the traps to avoid: oversized rooms, no camera control, finicky vehicles, objects wedging in geometry, and fetch-30-items busywork.

> **Source caveat:** Every research pass had full-page fetches blocked by the network proxy. **All findings come from search-engine excerpts and summaries, not full reads.** Quotes are as the excerpts gave them, so check them against the linked page before reusing them verbatim. Items marked **(unverified)** could not be traced to a specific outlet or primary source.

## Room density and scale: small, packed, one idea per room

- Good Job! has 9 themed floors (departments). Floors 1–7 have 4 stages each, about 32 stages in total, and "most … have unique objectives" ([NWR](http://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/53370/good-job-switch-review)).
- A stage takes 20–30 min on a first run and 5–8 min with practice. The game takes 4–5 h to finish ([GameFAQs](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/switch/286598-good-job/answers/542469-how-long-is-this-game), user-reported).
- Oversized stages were the complaint: "a few levels … much larger … a bit of a chore" at walking pace ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/good-job-review-office-space/1900-6417443/)).
- Density: "hundreds of physics-based destructible objects" per level. **(Unverified: the outlet could not be pinned down.)** My estimate of ~30–80 props per room is arithmetic on that unconfirmed number, not a count.
- Repeated carries are a trap: moving "around thirty items one-by-one" felt "repetitive to the point of being dull" **(outlet unverified)**.
- Captain Toad: Toad *can't jump* so that stages stay small. The director described the design as "packing a bento box" ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2014/12/interview_captain_toad_treasure_trackers_developers_talk_over_origins_and_the_contents_of_toads_backpack)).
- Odyssey built gimmicks first and added the scenery theme after, aiming for "a very great density of gameplay elements" ([Wikipedia citing interviews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_Odyssey)).
- Galaxy 2 capped Cloud Mario at 3 clouds so the mechanic couldn't break the levels ([Iwata Asks](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/supermariogalaxy2/1/0/)).

## Puzzle introduction and escalation: teach, develop, twist, conclude

- Hayashida's 4 steps: learn the mechanic, then a harder scenario, then "something crazy happens", then show mastery ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-structure-of-fun-learning-from-i-super-mario-3d-land-i-s-director)). The idea comes from Miyamoto's kishōtenketsu and his emphasis on the twist ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-secret-to-i-mario-i-level-design)).
- According to GMTK (a secondary source), a mechanic is "taught, developed, twisted and then thrown away in about five minutes flat" ([GMTK archive](https://archive.org/details/SuperMario3DWorlds4StepLevelDesignGameMakersToolkit)).
- World 1-1 teaches avoid, stomp, ? blocks, and good vs. bad mushroom with no text. The team "kept simulating what the player would do" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-miyamoto-built-i-super-mario-bros-i-legendary-world-1-1)).
- The first enemy started as a Koopa but became the simpler Goomba because the Koopa didn't teach well (same source).
- BotW's Great Plateau is a walled-off starter area. The exit is locked until 4 shrines teach the core abilities ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plateau)).
- Tezuka says creators underestimate difficulty, so get others' opinions and keep very hard content to "a fairly small part" of the game ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2015/10/takashi_tezuka_gives_his_opinion_on_hard_levels_made_with_super_mario_maker); [SMM2 tips](https://supermariomaker.nintendo.com/news/mr-tezuka-super-mario-maker-tips/)).
- Pikmin's *dandori* is planning parallel tasks under time and resource limits, balanced so jobs "finish … at about the same time" ([Miyamoto interview](https://nielsthooft.com/miyamoto-pikmin); [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/afterwords/2023/10/02/pikmin-4s-creators-on-why-development-isnt-only-about-making-miyamoto-happy)).

## Emergent physics play: clean path first, chaos layered on

- Paladin's process: prototype the "main experience", then build the layout and the **non-destructive** puzzle ("the most difficult thing to get right"), and only then add **destructive shortcuts** ([Nintendo Everything interview](https://nintendoeverything.com/good-job-interview-nintendo-paladin-studios/), excerpt).
- The star toy is the cable: drag it around corners, make it reach, and use it stretched as a slingshot that launches "cabinets and chairs" ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/good_job); [Eggplante](https://www.eggplante.com/2020/04/16/good-job-review/)).
- The same goal can be solved gently or loudly: a projector "wheeled gently through an electric door…or catapulted through the wall" ([Nintendo.com](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/good-job-switch/)).
- Physics pain points: items glitch through cords, forklifts are fiddly, objects wedge "between poles" **(the outlet for each quote is unverified)**. Also "things having a tendency to find themselves stuck" ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/good_job)).
- BotW's chemistry engine has 3 rules: elements change materials, elements change elements, materials don't change materials ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2017-03-12-breath-of-the-wild-gdc-talk.html)).
- TotK made *everything* physics-driven so interactions emerge "without anyone coding them by hand" ([Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/how-nintendo-designed-tears-of-the-kingdom-physics/)).
- Odyssey kept tester-found tricks: "If the dev team found a trick to be interesting, they might … put it into the game's specifications" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_Odyssey)).
- Splatoon's squid-hide mechanic came from an accident in a tofu-cube prototype ([Iwata Asks](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wiiu/splatoon/0/0/)).

## Humor and feedback: chaos is never punished

- Good Job! grades Time, Destruction and Repair cost, but the overall grade follows the *best* one: "two D's for destruction and an A for speed … still an A" ([NWR](http://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/53370/good-job-switch-review); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Job!)).
- A reviewer was "giggling … when launching workers through glass windows" ([Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/2020/04/good-job-review)).
- Breakables shatter like "an expensive crystal chandelier", backed by elevator-style music **(outlet unverified)**.
- Replay hooks: grade chasing plus 100+ hidden outfits ([Nintendo.com](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/good-job-switch/)).
- Nintendo aims for reactions: WarioWare wanted Iwata to say "This is ridiculous!" ([Iwata Asks](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-Wii/Iwata-Asks-WarioWare-Smooth-Moves/2-This-is-ridiculous-is-the-best-compliment/2-This-is-ridiculous-is-the-best-compliment-228514.html)). Wonder aimed to have a surprise in every course ([Ask the Developer 11](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-11-super-mario-bros-wonder-part-1/)).
- Labo showed an empty piano lid because "there's something really interesting" about seeing how it works; "Discover" is treated as equal to "Make" ([Labo interview Pt 2](https://www.nintendo.com/au/news-and-articles/nintendo-labo-developer-interview-part-2-prototyping-and-development/)).
- The Twinfinite reviewer found it "bland and samey" after the first hour or two ([Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/reviews/good-job-review/)), which shows why the objective has to keep changing.

## Minimal-text UI and camera: icons in, confusion and fixed cameras out

- Good Job! uses pop-up icons, warning-sign-pictogram people, a wordless intro, and no tutorials **(outlet unverified)**.
- Nintendo asked Paladin to "keep it simple" but "make sure it won't look plain" ([My Nintendo News](https://mynintendonews.com/2020/07/05/nintendo-and-paladin-studios-talk-about-their-relationship-on-good-job/)).
- The cost of no text: one reviewer thought the game "was glitched" when they just didn't understand a mechanic ([Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/2020/04/good-job-review)).
- The most repeated criticism was no camera control: "more challenging to survey larger levels" ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/good-job-review-office-space/1900-6417443/); [NWR](http://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/53370/good-job-switch-review)).
- Kirby counts a hit if it *looks* like a hit from the camera, which solves 3D depth ambiguity ([Ask the Developer 4](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-4-kirby-and-the-forgotten-land-part-2/)).
- WarioWare instructions are one verb plus a picture, e.g. "Pick!" ([Iwata Asks](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/ds/diy/0/3/)).
- BotW's "triangle rule" steers the eye with landmarks, reveals and tempo instead of text ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2017/10/zelda_breath_of_the_wilds_ingenious_design_is_all_about_triangles_apparently)).
- Captain Toad set out to "normalize the use of camera control" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Toad:_Treasure_Tracker)).

## Idea generation: diverge wildly, prototype cheaply, cut freely

- Wonder: 2,000+ unconditioned sticky notes from all disciplines, then "prototype meetings" to pick which to build ([Ask the Developer 11](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-11-super-mario-bros-wonder-part-1/)). Sources disagree on whether these were general ideas or Wonder effects specifically ([KitGuru](https://www.kitguru.net/tech-news/mustafa-mahmoud/super-mario-bros-wonder-originally-had-over-2000-different-effects/)).
- Other bare-minimum prototypes: a block room for Mario 64 movement ([shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/mario64/)); a gravity sphere for Galaxy ([Iwata Asks](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/super_mario_galaxy/0/0/)); a top-down NES-style build to test BotW physics ([Zelda Universe](https://zeldauniverse.net/2017/03/09/zelda-team-discusses-breath-of-the-wilds-development-and-2d-zelda-prototype-at-gdc/)).
- Good Job! itself started as a stick-figure, black-and-white prototype ([GoNintendo](https://www.gonintendo.com/stories/364413-nintendo-explains-how-their-collaboration-with-paladin-studios-fo)).
- WarioWare pitches can be one drawing and one word, and programmers pick the ones that look easy to build ([Iwata Asks](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/ds/diy/0/3/)).
- Cutting is normal. For 3D World, "no 'upending the tea table' this time" was called unusual ([Iwata Asks](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wiiu/super-mario-3d-world/0/0/)). 3D Land cut several prototyped gimmicks ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-i-super-mario-3d-land-i-from-adversity-to-joy)).

## What to put into Circuit Crew, in priority order

**P0: core, do first**
1. **A grey-box feel prototype:** one room, the character, one springy cable and one plug. Tune grab, carry, drag and snap before building any puzzles.
2. **The cable as the star:** limited length, wraps around corners, visible tension (colour or stretch). When pulled taut it slingshots props.
3. **Forgiving connection:** a plug connects when it *looks* connected from the camera (Kirby rule), with generous snap radii. Objective items auto-nudge or respawn if wedged.
4. **Player camera control:** rotate and zoom, plus auto-framing on larger rooms. This fixes Good Job!'s top complaint.
5. **Clean solution first, chaos second:** ship each room with a guaranteed tidy route (cable through doors, breaker in order) before adding shortcuts (smash a wall, slingshot the generator).
6. **Small diorama rooms:** a target of 5–10 min per room, one screen or a few linked rooms. No room should need a long walk. Carrying a heavy item slows the player instead of letting them jump (the Captain Toad constraint).

**P1: structure and teaching**
7. **One circuit idea per room, taught in 4 beats:** safe → pressured → twist (flip the role, e.g. a capacitor goes from power source to shock hazard) → showcase flourish (the lights come on).
8. **A "Great Plateau" starter wing:** 3–4 mini-rooms, each teaching one verb (carry, route, breaker reset, capacitor). Completing them unlocks the main facility.
9. **The first room as World 1-1:** a dead lamp, a visibly broken wire, one part nearby. Pair two look-alikes early (live vs. safe cable, breaker vs. switch).
10. **Wings in the Good Job! structure:** themed facility wings (power, server room, lab…), 3–4 jobs each, promotion to unlock the next. Change the objective every job.
11. **A small, published rule table** in the style of BotW chemistry: power changes device state; water on a live cable spreads current; a capacitor stores and releases; cables only affect each other when joined. Every interactive object is a real state or physics object, not scripted.
12. **Caps on strong verbs** per room (cable length, splitter count, capacitor charges) so no single tool bypasses the puzzle.
13. **Power budget as dandori:** sequencing puzzles such as charge → carry → flip before it drains. Self-running powered devices (e.g. a conveyor) act as "helpers" in solo play.

**P2: humor, feedback, UI, process**
14. **Scoring that doesn't judge:** separate Time / Damage / Parts-wasted grades, with the headline grade set by the best one. Damage is shown as a comically large repair bill.
15. **Every prop reacts:** sparks, pops, flickering bulbs, staff who flinch or cheer. Failure is comic (frizzed hair, a room-wide blackout gag), never a lecture. Breakables should sound *expensive*.
16. **Icon-first UI:** one verb plus an icon per room ("Connect!" with a plug icon). Highlight grabbable and pluggable objects so players never mistake missing knowledge for a bug.
17. **Labo-style "Discover":** open panels or an x-ray view that shows current flowing, so the explanation is a toy rather than text.
18. **One surprise per room** (a Wonder moment): a hidden switch changes the room's rules briefly and leads to a collectible.
19. **Avoid busywork:** no carrying 30 identical items one by one. Batch them with a cart, or use fewer, more interesting parts. Keep vehicles simple or skip them.
20. **Process rituals:** a timed no-rules sticky-note jam with every discipline, where each idea is one picture plus one word. Hold a weekly prototype pick of 2–3 ideas. Name each room for its idea before building it. Run blind playtests and log laughs and retries. Keep good emergent exploits as intended solutions.
